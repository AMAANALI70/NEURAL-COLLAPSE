# C. Current Experiment Findings

## Available Evidence
**2 conditions × 1 seed × 30 epochs × natural HAM10000 distribution (ratio ≈ 1)**

| Metric | ETF head (s=42) | Linear baseline (s=42) | Δ (ETF − Linear) |
|--------|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Best val accuracy | 53.1% | 71.9% | **−18.8%** |
| Macro F1 | 0.203 | 0.216 | −0.013 |
| Mean sensitivity | 25.3% | 27.4% | −2.1% |
| Cohen's Kappa | 0.111 | 0.272 | −0.161 |
| ROC-AUC | 0.744 | 0.761 | −0.017 |
| NC1 (final) | 16.04 | 12.98 | +3.06 |
| NC2 (final) | 1.244 | 1.128 | +0.116 |
| NC3 (final) | 0.966 | 0.834 | +0.132 |
| NC4 (final) | 0.580 | 0.560 | +0.020 |

## Finding F1: ETF Accuracy vs Linear Accuracy
**ETF underperforms linear by ~19 percentage points in validation accuracy.** However, accuracy is dominated by Nevi (67% of val). Linear achieves this by heavily predicting Nevi — its Nevi recall is 92.5% vs ETF's 68.6%. This is the classic accuracy/sensitivity trade-off under imbalance.

## Finding F2: Melanoma Recall
**Melanoma (rarest malignant class, 182 val samples) recall:**  
- ETF: **15.4%**  
- Linear: **3.3%**  

**ETF detects Melanoma 4.7× more often than the linear baseline.** This is the single most important clinical finding so far and directly supports the ETF-geometry hypothesis. However, it is based on 1 seed and natural distribution only.

## Finding F3: Tail-Class Collapse
**Both methods fail on DF (17 samples) and Vascular (12 samples):**  
- Linear: DF recall = 0%, Vascular recall = 0%  
- ETF: DF recall = 5.9%, Vascular recall = 50% (but precision = 1.9% — random-ish)  

The ETF Vascular result (50% recall, 1.9% precision) may reflect collapse artifact rather than learned geometry.

## Finding F4: NC1 Behavior (Epoch-by-Epoch)
**NC1 (within-class scatter) is high and volatile for both methods:**  
- ETF NC1 range: 4.7–16.5 (high variance across epochs; no clean downward trend)  
- Linear NC1 range: 7.3–26.1 (worse, more variable)  

Neither method achieves NC1 < 1 at 30 epochs with natural HAM10000. **Neural Collapse is not occurring at this training length/learning rate.** NC1 is well above zero — the geometry has not collapsed. This is expected for a real-world imbalanced dataset but is a critical observation: the framework is measuring pre-collapse geometry, not classical NC.

## Finding F5: NC4 (NCC Disagreement)
**NC4 (fraction where argmax ≠ NCC) is 0.39–0.58 for both:**  
This is high — in collapsed ETF geometry, NC4 → 0. The argmax and nearest-class-center classifiers substantially disagree. This confirms we are far from the idealized NC regime. The ETF head has NC4 ≈ 0.39 at epoch 30, trending down (from 0.89 at epoch 1), suggesting slow progress toward geometric collapse.

## Finding F6: NC2 Under ETF Head
**NC2 (ETF cosine deviation) for ETF head at epoch 30 = 1.244.** For a perfect ETF structure, NC2 → 0. This is not converging to zero in 30 epochs on HAM10000. The ETF head guarantees the *classifier geometry* is fixed ETF, but the *feature geometry* (class means) is not converging to that structure. This is the key tension the thesis must address.

## Finding F7: Training Instability
**Val accuracy for ETF oscillates dramatically:**  
Epoch-by-epoch: 3.1% → 13.8% → 43.1% → 48.1% → 29.4% → 10.6% → ... → peak 53.1% at epoch 8.  
This is not smooth convergence. The fixed ETF head may cause gradient instability. The baseline is similarly volatile but converges faster (71.9% at epoch 11, then regresses to ~52%).

# D. Research Gaps

## Critical Gaps (block research claims)

| Gap | Why Critical |
|-----|-------------|
| **No imbalance progression data** | Cannot describe "how NC geometry behaves under long-tail imbalance" without sweeping ratio=1→5→10→20→50 |
| **No multi-seed validation** | Every result is single-seed. No mean ± std. No statistical claims possible. |
| **Bug in `imbalance_study.py` line 80** | `cfg_copy` dict created but `dataset.imbalance_ratio` never set in it before calling `get_medical_dataloaders`. The study will always run at natural distribution regardless of `ratio` argument. |
| **No rebalancing comparison** | Cannot identify "best minority-class remedy" without comparing focal, weighted_ce, oversampling, ETF at same ratio |
| **No trend plots** | Raw data in CSVs but no NC1-vs-ratio or sensitivity-vs-ratio curves generated |

## Important Gaps (needed for complete story)

| Gap | Why Important |
|-----|--------------|
| **Melanoma recall vs imbalance ratio** | The key clinical curve — how does Melanoma detection degrade as imbalance grows? |
| **NC1 comparison plot (ETF vs Linear over epochs)** | Core thesis visual — geometry evolution side by side |
| **NC regularization effect on minority recall** | Does adding λ·L_collapse improve Melanoma sensitivity? |
| **Pretrained vs scratch** | Should confirm pretrained is better; currently assumed |

## Non-Gaps (do NOT pursue)

- UMAP visualization — redundant with t-SNE
- Architecture ablation (ResNet vs MobileNet) — not relevant to NC geometry question
- ETF scale hyper-parameter tuning — secondary to establishing main effects first
- Additional datasets (ChestXRay, RetinalOCT) — the research question is sufficiently hard on HAM10000
